# Code of Ethics

VoiceLK's goal is to support accessible Sinhala education through responsible speech technology. These principles guide project decisions, contributions, and research. They do not amend the [MIT License](LICENSE) or third-party license terms.

## Consent and provenance

Use recordings and text only with appropriate rights for the intended training, evaluation, and distribution. Record source, permission, license, attribution, and intended use. Publicly available audio is not proof of speaker consent or permission to train.

For new voice collections, explain how recordings and models will be used and shared before collecting consent. Document a contact and withdrawal process, including any limits once artifacts have been distributed. Use suitable guardian consent and participant assent processes when working with minors.

Do not publish private consent forms or personal identifiers. Resolve uncertain permissions before accepting affected data or releasing derived artifacts.

## Voice identity and disclosure

Do not contribute demonstrations intended to impersonate someone deceptively, commit fraud, harass people, or bypass voice authentication. Label generated speech clearly in demonstrations and research outputs. Do not suggest that a real speaker said or endorsed synthetic content.

## Privacy and educational use

Minimize collection of student information, private text, and identifiable voice data. Keep research evidence separate from public artifacts and use synthetic examples in issues.

Have educators or competent language reviewers check high-impact instructional content. Preserve access to the original text and a way to report incorrect speech. Do not present the system as a substitute for human judgment.

## Language inclusion

Evaluate Sinhala, English, and mixed-language content, including ICT terminology, numbers, and abbreviations. Report limitations across dialects, speakers, recording conditions, and user groups. Avoid equating a single accent with correctness.

## Research integrity

Report methods and limitations honestly. Preserve dataset splits, checkpoint identity, seeds, and metric configurations. Do not claim improvement from incomparable runs, selected examples alone, or metrics that were not successfully computed.

Document data leakage risks and use human listening feedback alongside suitable objective metrics. Credit datasets, software, contributors, and prior research.

## Stewardship and accountability

Use compute proportionately and reuse validated artifacts when suitable. Review privacy, accessibility, misuse, and distribution implications before releasing datasets or models.

Raise non-sensitive ethical concerns through a feature or general issue. Follow the [Code of Conduct](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) for harmful behavior and [Security policy](SECURITY.md) for vulnerabilities or exposure of private information.

Maintainers should investigate credible concerns, document corrective actions without exposing private information, and pause affected contributions or releases while unresolved consent or provenance questions are examined.
